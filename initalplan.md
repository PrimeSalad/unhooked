UNHOOKED
CATEGORY – AI in Health
Unhooked is an AI-powered personal wellness assistant that helps users build healthier habits around debt, spending, and screen time through personalized insights, practical recommendations, and timely interventions.
Debt: Track money owed or lent, organize repayment records and harassment evidence, create payoff plans, and receive personalized guidance on managing debt.
Spend: Evaluate planned purchases, check affordability, compare alternatives, calculate total BNPL costs, and pause impulse buying with a 24-hour cooling period.
Scroll: Recognize excessive scrolling, receive gentle check-ins, and get personalized suggestions for healthier screen-time habits.
1. Problem
People face growing financial and mental wellness challenges due to unmanaged debt, impulsive purchases, buy-now-pay-later (BNPL) services, and excessive social media use.
Many struggle to manage money they owe or are owed, determine whether a purchase is affordable, or recognize when scrolling becomes an unhealthy habit. Aggressive digital platforms and predatory lending practices can make these challenges worse.
Existing tools often focus on tracking problems rather than helping people make better decisions at the moment they need support.
2. Proposed Solution
Unhooked is an AI-powered personal health and behavioral wellness assistant that helps users recognize unhealthy patterns, understand their options, and make more intentional decisions.
Its main approach is Trigger → AI Pause → Reflection → Recommendation → Decision.
The app offers three core modules:
Debt Management: Organize debts, track money owed and lent, plan repayments, document harassment, and receive guidance on possible next steps.
Smart Spending: Evaluate planned purchases, check affordability, compare alternatives, understand installment costs, and delay impulsive purchases.
Mindful Scrolling: Recognize extended scrolling sessions, deliver gentle interventions, and encourage healthier alternatives.
Rather than simply blocking behavior, Unhooked helps users understand why they might want to act and gives them practical options before they decide.
3. Impact
Unhooked aims to help users:
Improve awareness of their financial obligations and spending habits.
Reduce impulsive borrowing and unnecessary purchases.
Organize debt records and preserve evidence of abusive collection practices.
Develop more intentional screen-time habits.
Reduce avoidable financial stress and digital fatigue.
Build healthier long-term behavioral patterns through small, achievable decisions.

2. AI Pause Intervention
This is the central feature of Unhooked.
Whenever a user approaches a potentially risky decision, the app introduces a short pause, helps them reflect, and offers relevant recommendations.
Flow:
Trigger → AI Pause → Reflection → Recommendation → Decision
Examples include considering a new loan, preparing to make a large purchase, or spending an extended period scrolling.
Each intervention can include:
A short countdown.
A brief AI-generated reflection.
Context-specific insights.
Personalized suggestions.
Continue, reconsider, or take-a-break options.
Example: Before borrowing
"You already have repayments coming up. Would taking another loan make your next few weeks harder?"
The user can review their obligations, adjust their borrowing plan, or continue with their decision.
The goal is not to control the user but to create space for a more informed choice.

Scrolling insight

RESEARCH BRIEF — AI IN HEALTH
10. Research Overview
Debt, impulsive spending, and problematic social media use can overlap with stress and mental health challenges.
Unhooked addresses these behaviors by introducing support at the moment a user is considering a decision or engaging in a potentially unhealthy habit.
Rather than relying only on tracking and reminders, the app combines brief pauses, personalized recommendations, and behavioral insights to help users make more intentional choices.
Health and behavioral concerns
Debt: Financial pressure, repayment difficulties, and abusive collection practices can contribute to psychological distress.
Spend: Impulsive purchases and installment commitments can create financial pressure, particularly when users underestimate their total obligations.
Scroll: Compulsive social media use is associated with poorer mental health outcomes, although the relationship varies by person and usage pattern.
These relationships do not mean every person with debt, frequent purchases, or long screen time has a mental health problem.
11. Debt and Mental Health
Debt can affect wellbeing when repayments become difficult, financial uncertainty increases, or lenders use threatening collection practices.
Global context
Research has associated personal debt with mental health difficulties, including depression and anxiety. However, observational evidence does not establish that debt alone causes these conditions.
Philippine context
Abusive online lending practices can involve threatening messages, public shaming, and misuse of personal information.
Users experiencing harassment may need a way to preserve messages and payment records while seeking appropriate assistance.
What Unhooked does
Helps users organize money owed and money lent.
Tracks repayment progress and upcoming obligations.
Provides realistic repayment-planning tools.
Preserves potentially relevant harassment evidence.
Encourages users to review existing obligations before borrowing again.
12. Impulse Spending and BNPL
Buy-now-pay-later services can make purchases appear more affordable by emphasizing individual installments rather than the full amount owed.
Users may also make purchases without accounting for other financial commitments.
Key concerns
Underestimating total repayment costs.
Accumulating multiple installment obligations.
Spending beyond a planned budget.
Making purchases in response to stress or temporary emotions.
What Unhooked does
Displays the estimated total cost of installment purchases.
Evaluates purchases against a user-defined budget.
Suggests lower-cost alternatives.
Encourages users to wait before making nonessential purchases.
Helps users consider upcoming bills and repayments.
The goal is to make the financial consequences clearer before the user commits.
13. Doomscrolling and Mental Health
Extended or compulsive scrolling can interfere with sleep, concentration, daily responsibilities, and wellbeing.
However, screen time alone does not determine whether social media use is harmful. The user's intentions, activities, and personal circumstances matter.
What Unhooked does
Tracks scrolling sessions where technically supported.
Identifies sessions that exceed user-defined limits.
Offers gentle check-ins rather than automatically blocking access.
Recommends short breaks and alternative activities.
Helps users reflect on their habits over time.
The app focuses on encouraging intentional use rather than treating all social media activity as negative.
14. Why the AI Pause Approach
Unhooked uses short, timely interventions to help users reconsider decisions before acting.
Friction before action
A brief delay can create an opportunity to reflect on an impulse.
Unhooked applies this approach through:
Borrowing pauses.
Checkout pauses.
Purchase cooling periods.
Scrolling check-ins.
Just-in-time recommendations
Instead of delivering generic advice at fixed intervals, the app aims to provide support when it is relevant to the user's activity.
For example:
A user considering a new loan receives a summary of existing obligations.
A user planning a purchase receives an affordability estimate.
A user in an extended scrolling session receives a gentle check-in.
Recommendations should be based on available data, and the user should retain control over the final decision.
15. Why On-Device AI and Privacy Matter
Unhooked may handle sensitive information, including financial records, personal messages, behavioral patterns, and wellbeing check-ins.
The design should prioritize data minimization and transparency.
Privacy principles
Store sensitive records locally where feasible.
Request only the permissions required for each feature.
Explain how data is used before requesting access.
Let users delete their records.
Avoid collecting contacts or messages without explicit authorization.
Use secure processing and storage.
Clearly disclose whenever data must be sent to an external AI service.
On-device processing can reduce the need to transmit sensitive information, but it should not be described as fully private unless the implementation supports that claim.
16. Responsible AI
Unhooked is a self-help and behavioral wellness tool, not a medical diagnosis or treatment service.
Its AI should:
Provide practical suggestions instead of making decisions for users.
Distinguish facts, estimates, and recommendations.
Avoid shame-based messaging.
Avoid guaranteeing financial outcomes.
Allow users to correct inaccurate information.
Direct users to qualified professionals or verified support resources when needed.

21. Conclusion
Unhooked helps people pause, understand their options, and make more intentional decisions.

Its central idea is simple: instead of only tracking problems after they happen, Unhooked helps users make better-informed choices before small impulses become bigger problems.



Unhooked — Research Brief (AI in Health)
Summary
Debt, impulse spending, and doomscrolling are health problems, not just money or habit problems. Each is consistently linked in peer-reviewed research to depression, anxiety, and stress, and unsecured debt is linked to sharply higher suicide risk.
Unhooked targets the moment before each behavior. This design is based on a published field experiment in which a short pause before opening an app reduced app openings by 57% over six weeks. Unhooked applies the same mechanism to borrowing, buying, and scrolling, using an on-device AI that keeps sensitive data private.

Debt and Mental Health
Debt is one of the strongest social risk factors for poor mental health, and abusive online lending makes it worse in the Philippines.
Global evidence
A meta-analysis in Clinical Psychology Review (52 papers) found debt linked to mental disorder (odds ratio 3.24), depression (2.77), and suicide completion (7.9). The authors note that causality is hard to establish.
A systematic review of Asian studies found debt related to depression, anxiety, stress, and suicidal ideation, with hopelessness partly explaining the link to suicide.
Philippine context
PAOCC received 47,446 complaints about abusive online lending apps between August 2024 and January 2026, and officials cited reports of borrowers suffering from depression.
Victims reported threats, public shaming, and contacts accessed without permission. The PNP advised them to save screenshots as evidence and to report.
What Unhooked does: It turns scattered screenshots into an organized evidence pack, lowers the burden of reporting, and adds a pause before new borrowing.
Impulse Spending and BNPL
Buy-now-pay-later and mental health appear to feed each other: people in distress use it more, and the resulting debt can deepen the distress.
Global evidence
A JAMA Health Forum study of US adults found that people reporting depression symptoms had 1.91 times the odds of using BNPL. The authors suggest the link may run both ways.
UK research by Money and Mental Health found that 43% of BNPL users felt it pushed them to spend more than they could afford. It also noted that impulsivity and disorganization linked to mental health problems make repayments harder.
Philippine context
In an UnaCash survey, the average BNPL purchase was about 42% of respondents' monthly income, and 48% used BNPL at least monthly.
56.4% of Filipinos buy online every week.
What Unhooked does: It shows the full repayment total at checkout instead of only the small first installment, and adds a 24-hour cooling-off period.
Doomscrolling and Mental Health
How people use social media matters more than raw hours, and compulsive scrolling is the pattern most strongly tied to poor mental health.
Global evidence
A meta-analysis of 18 studies (9,269 young people) found problematic social media use moderately correlated with depression (r = 0.27), anxiety (r = 0.35), and stress (r = 0.31).
The same review notes that addiction-like use predicts depressive symptoms better than time spent online. This is why Unhooked looks at scrolling patterns, not just a timer.
A 2026 scoping review describes doomscrolling as a compulsive habit reinforced by infinite scroll and algorithmic feeds, consistently associated with anxiety, depression, stress, and reduced resilience.
Philippine context
Filipinos spend about 54 hours a week online, the second highest in the world, and about 4.8 hours a day on social platforms.
What Unhooked does: It detects mindless scrolling patterns and offers a gentle check-in instead of a hard block.
Why the Approach Works
Unhooked combines two evidence-backed techniques: a short pause at the moment of the urge, and support delivered just in time.
Technique
Evidence
How Unhooked uses it
Friction (a short delay before acting)
In a PNAS field study, the one sec app's pop-up led users to close the target app 36% of the time and cut app openings by 57% after six weeks. The time delay worked; a message alone did not.
Timed pause cards before borrowing, at checkout, and during long scroll sessions
Long-term friction
A CHI 2024 study of 1,039 one sec users over about 13 weeks found friction reduced open attempts and made app use more intentional over time.
Pauses stay light so people keep them on
Just-in-time adaptive interventions (JITAIs)
A 2023 meta-analysis found a medium effect on objective behavior change (g = 0.77). A 2025 BMJ Mental Health meta-analysis of 23 studies found a small but lasting benefit for mental health (g = 0.15), up to six months.
The AI decides when to step in (a new lending app, a BNPL checkout, a scroll pattern) instead of sending fixed reminders

Design implication: The pause must include a real time delay, not just a message, since the PNAS experiment found that the delay drove the effect.
Why Local AI
The people who need Unhooked most are the least likely to seek help or share their data, so the AI runs on the device.
Stigma and cost block help-seeking. Philippine mental health providers ranked affordability and stigma as the top barriers to care. Studies put formal help-seeking at only 2.2% to 17.5% of Filipinos with mental health problems.
Professional care is scarce. The country has about 0.6 psychiatrists per 100,000 people, against a global average of 1.7, and most are in Metro Manila. A private, always-available first step helps fill part of that gap.
The data is legally sensitive. The Data Privacy Act of 2012 classifies health information as sensitive personal information. Keeping screenshots, spending, and wellbeing data on the device avoids collecting it at all.
Responsible-AI note: Unhooked is a self-help tool, not a diagnosis or treatment. Every pause card links to professional help, such as the NCMH crisis hotline.
